## 3. What I found: our extractor was hiding the stream-on sequence

I went to the blob before opening Ghidra, and the answer was in it.

The blob has **14 `regSetting` arrays**. Our extractor produced **7 tables**. Its
alignment probe needs a full entry *after* a skew to test the constant signature,
so in a 40-byte region every non-zero skew has nothing to look at — and these
arrays carry their signature two words in. Seven single-entry arrays were dropped
without a word. Four are exactly what you have been hunting:

```
{0x0100, 0x01}   stream ON        {0x0104, 0x01}   grouped parameter hold ON
{0x0100, 0x00}   stream OFF       {0x0104, 0x00}   hold OFF
```

**The vendor sequence is hold, then the mode registers, then release, then stream
on. Our driver does mode registers then stream on, with no hold.**

This also retires a claim the driver stated as fact — that the blob never writes
`0x0100` and the Windows stack does it itself. It writes it. That sentence was
describing a hole in our tooling, not the firmware.

`probes/chromatix_regs.py` is fixed and `data/imx681-registers.txt` regenerated
(11 tables, 905 writes). The ov13858 blob gained a table too, which suggests the
fix is general rather than tuned to one file.

**Tension with your measurement, stated plainly.** You found `0x0104` reads 0 and
rejects writes. If that holds, the hold cannot be the mechanism and this lead
dies. But your probe ran while streaming and while idle — not at the moment the
vendor uses it, which is before the mode table with the sensor freshly powered.
That is why item 4 is specific about when.

## 4. Your two open questions, answered from firmware

**Section 5, the third rail: there isn't one.** The `_RES` power blobs list every
rail Windows votes for, per sensor:

| sensor | rails |
|---|---|
| **CAMF (front, IMX681)** | **LDO3_M 1.8 V, LDO7_B 2.8 V — two, no core** |
| CAMS (rear) | LDO6_M 1.8, **LDO1_M 1.2**, LDO5_M 2.8, LDO16_B 2.9 |
| CAMI (IR) | LDO4_M 1.8, **LDO2_M 1.15**, LDO7_M 2.8 |

The other two sensors do get a core rail and the front one does not. Windows
powers this sensor with exactly the two rails our DT declares, so your
counter-evidence was the right read: the IMX681 generates its core internally.
**`vreg_l3c_0p8` is a dead end — do not spend a boot on it.**

**Reset GPIO 237 is corroborated.** Your DSDT search was sound but looking in the
wrong file: the reset line is in the DSDT for *none* of the three sensors. It is
in `CAMF_RES_MSHW0490.bin` as `TLMMGPIO 0xed (237)`, in the power-on sequence,
asserted before the rails and released after. Independently,
`x1-dell-thena.dtsi` uses gpio237 for its own front camera. Two sources, one of
them this machine's firmware. It was never an uncorroborated number — the
provenance lived in `docs/camera-sensors.md` rather than in the commit message.

## 5. Ground rules that have already cost us

1. **Never report a failed write without a positive control in the same run.**
   You established this with `0x0100`; it is project doctrine now.
2. **i2c adapter numbers are not stable across boots.** The sensor was `5-0010`
   on Aug 4 and `1-0010` since. Identify the bus by the EEPROM at `0x50`.
3. **`STATE[after-mode]` showing zeros is expected** — it runs before the control
   handler and before stream-on. Only `STATE[streaming]` decides anything.
4. **The bootloader goes on the T7, never the internal NVMe.** If you ever mount
   an ESP: `EFI/Microsoft/` present means it is the wrong one.
